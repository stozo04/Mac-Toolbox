# AGENTS.md

Quick orientation for any coding agent working in this repo.

## What this is
Toolbox: a native macOS app (SwiftUI, macOS 14+, Swift Package Manager) that bundles many small utilities (video, audio, images, docs, text & speech, files, Mac) in one window. Everything is meant to run on device. The designs are screenshots in `images/` (`images/All.png` is the home screen).

**Current state:** navigation, catalog, theme (dark/light toggle), logging with a Logs page and generic empty-state workspaces exist. Thirty-seven tools are built: two in Audio (Trim Audio, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Audio/TrimAudio/`: one file, a waveform with draggable handles, fade in/out heard before saving, saved by the bundled ffmpeg as the same format (copied as is, or rewritten losslessly for WAV/AIFF/FLAC) or as MP3, M4A, WAV, FLAC or AIFF; Extract Audio, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Audio/ExtractAudio/`: the first soundtrack of each video saved as M4A, MP3, WAV or AIFF; AAC is copied losslessly with ffmpeg, MP3 is encoded by the LAME library built into the bundled ffmpeg by `scripts/fetch-tools.sh`, WAV/AIFF and other AAC are made with AVFoundation), eleven in Images (Convert Images, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Images/ConvertImages/`, with the vendored WebP encoder in `Sources/CWebP/`; Compress Images, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Images/CompressImages/`, built on the Convert Images encoder; Blur Faces, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Images/BlurFaces/`, Vision face detection; Beautify Screenshot, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Images/BeautifyScreenshot/`; Resize Images, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Images/ResizeImages/`, by percentage, longest side or exact size, with a preview whose handles you drag to set the size; Rotate Images, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Images/RotateImages/`, 90 degree turns and flips for a whole batch with a live preview of the result; Remove Background, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Images/RemoveBackground/`; Remove Metadata, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Images/RemoveMetadata/`, lossless JPEG copy plus a verify pass; Redact Image, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Images/RedactImage/`, blur, pixelate or black box over regions you drag or Find & Cover finds with Vision; Annotate Screenshot, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Images/AnnotateScreenshot/`, one image at a time with arrows, shapes, text, numbered steps, pen, blur and crop, copy/save/drag-out, paste and Capture Area; Color Picker, `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Images/ColorPicker/`, samples any pixel with `NSColorSampler` (no screen recording permission) and copies it as HEX, rgb(), hsl(), OKLCH, SwiftUI, NSColor, UIColor or the nearest Tailwind color, keeps the last 20 colors, finds 5 to 8 main colors of an image and checks a text/background pair against WCAG 2), ten in Video (Download Video, Record Screen, Trim Video, Video to GIF, Resize Video, Compress Video, Extract Frames, Media Info, Mute Video, Burn In Subtitles; code in `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Video/`, each routed from `ToolWorkspace` through its own `ToolInput` case) and all fourteen in Mac (code in `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/Mac/<Tool>/`, routed through the single `ToolInput.mac(MacTool)` case and `MacToolView`; shared pieces are `Tools/Mac/MacUI.swift`, `Support/Shell.swift`, `Support/SystemDefaults.swift`, `Support/CodeSignInfo.swift`, `Support/DirectorySize.swift`). Two cards open other free apps instead of a Toolbox tool (`ToolInput.companionApp`, `Models/CompanionApp.swift`, `Views/CompanionAppView.swift`): **Folio** is the whole Docs category (PDFs) and **Scarlet** sits in Video beside its tools (AI video editing). Clicking one opens the app when it is installed, found by bundle ID through Launch Services; otherwise its page links to where the app's builds are published (Folio's are in `mberman84/folio-releases`, not its source repository). The **AI Models** page is real too (`Views/ModelsView.swift`, logic in `Sources/Toolbox/AIModels/`): it lists the models on this Mac with what each does (a folder of `.safetensors` shards beside a `config.json` or similar is one model), its location and the apps that use it, keeps itself current while the page is open (FSEvents on the folders models live in, Spotlight for loose files, a look when the app comes forward, at most once every 30 seconds; all of it stops when you leave the page), and can gather movable models into `~/Library/Application Support/Toolbox/AI Models`, leaving a symlink behind (checked against DigUp's own helper; models of other apps, the Hugging Face cache and Ollama are never moved). The **Vault** page is real (`Sources/Toolbox/Vault/`, brief in `features/Vault/README.md`): a password-protected, hidden library of photos and videos stored in an AES-256 encrypted disk image that Finder also only opens with the password; files are moved in (copied, checked, original deleted), and a viewer steps through them with the arrow keys. Download Video's "Add to Vault" can open it with Touch ID: when "Use Touch ID to add downloads" is ticked at unlock, a copy of the password is kept in `TouchID.key` beside the image, sealed to a Secure Enclave key that only the current fingerprints unlock (`Vault/VaultTouchIDKey.swift`); a vault a send opened is locked again when the send ends. Smart search finds them by what they show or say, with EmbeddingGemma 2 on llama.cpp (`Sources/VaultSearch/`, adapted from DigUp), its index kept inside the vault. Every other tool still shows the generic empty state and does not process anything. Building a dedicated, design-accurate view per tool is the main remaining work. Network code exists only for Download Video (the downloads, and "Update yt-dlp", which fetches the program and its checksum file from yt-dlp's GitHub release), Network Speed Test (Apple's `networkQuality`), the Vault's one-time search model download. Media Info's "Open in Maps" makes no request itself: it asks macOS to open a `maps.apple.com` link.

## Run / build
Apple silicon only. Build llama.cpp once first (Vault search; needs CMake): `scripts/build-llama.sh`.
```
scripts/build-llama.sh
swift build
swift run Toolbox
.build/debug/Toolbox --page "Video/Trim Video"   # debug: open a page at launch (home | <Category> | <Category>/<Tool>)
.build/debug/Toolbox --release-notes             # debug: open the release history at launch
.build/debug/Toolbox --vault-folder /tmp/vault   # debug builds only: keep the Vault page's vault in this folder
.build/debug/Toolbox --log-store /tmp/logs.sqlite   # debug builds only: read and write this log file
```
A development run (a debug build, or anything run from `.build/`) keeps its vault in `~/Library/Application Support/Toolbox/.vault-dev.noindex`; only the installed app uses the real one (`VaultLocation.launch`).

## Code map
- `Sources/Toolbox/Models/ToolCatalog.swift` - single source of truth for every tool (name, category, SF Symbol, tagline, workspace type). Add or rename tools here.
- `Sources/Toolbox/Models/Tool.swift`, `ToolCategory.swift` - models; `ToolInput` picks the generic workspace.
- `Sources/Toolbox/Support/ReleaseNotes.swift` - app version and release history; `Views/VersionBadge.swift` shows them.
- `Sources/Toolbox/Support/AppState.swift` - page, search, favorites, recents, dark/light. `Theme.swift` - adaptive colors (use `Theme.*`, never hardcode white/black).
- `Sources/Toolbox/AIModels/` - `ModelScanner` (where models are and what they are), `ModelKnowledge` (plain-words descriptions), `GGUFInfo`, `AppAssociations` (which app uses a model), `ModelLibrary` (the live list: `FileWatcher` + Spotlight), `ModelMover` (central folder with links).
- `Sources/Toolbox/Vault/` - `VaultDoor` (the one owner of the encrypted image: every `diskutil`/`hdiutil` call, one at a time, and the lock file that says which copy has it open), `VaultDisk` (what can be read from the disk without running a program), `VaultQuitGuard` (lock before quitting), `VaultMover` (verified move in), `VaultLibrary`, `VaultThumbnails`, `VaultModel` (the page's state; its duplicates actions are in `VaultModelDuplicates.swift`, what Download Video asks of it in `VaultModelDownloads.swift`, and it keeps private state in small owned types instead of growing: `VaultMoveTracker` for the move that is running and what an outside close did to it, `VaultDuplicateFinder` for the digests and looks behind the Duplicates view), and the door, library and viewer views. `Vault/Search/` - `VaultSearchEngine`, `VaultIndex` (SQLite inside the vault), `VaultRanking`, `VaultModelDownload`, `VaultSearchBar`, `VaultSearchWaiters` (who waits for an item to be indexed), `VaultIndexQueue` (the items the index takes first). `Vault/VaultTouchIDKey.swift` - the vault password sealed to a Secure Enclave key for Download Video's Add to Vault.
- `Sources/VaultSearch/` + `Sources/CLlama/` - EmbeddingGemma 2 on llama.cpp (`LlamaEmbedder`), media readers and scoring, adapted from DigUp (MIT). Not linted (kept close to DigUp's code).
- `Sources/Toolbox/Support/Logging/` - `Log` (the facade and its sinks), `LogDetails` (`Log.details`, `LogText` scrubbing), `LogEvent` (with its `signature`), `LogStore` (`logs.sqlite`: the `logs` table, with `archived_at` for logs hidden on the Logs page, and the `log_resolutions` table), `LogStoreSink`/`LogStoreLocation` (which file a run writes). `Sources/Toolbox/Logs/` - the Logs page: `LogsModel` (range, levels, status, counts), `LogStatus` (what the log fixer's decision means for one event, and `VersionNumber`), `LogsView`, `LogDetailView`. `Support/SQLiteDatabase.swift` - the one SQLite connection helper (`VaultIndex` and `LogStore` use it).
- `Sources/Toolbox/Support/BatchQueue.swift` - the one queue behind every tool that works through a list of files into a dated folder: `BatchQueue<Item>` (add with skipped notice and duplicates, remove, clear, the run loop with its run token, cancel, retry of failed items, progress, the output folder, reveal), the `BatchStatus`/`BatchItem` protocols a tool's own `Status` and `Item` adopt, `BatchTool` (folder name, log category, wording, which files count) and `BatchQueueModel` (the forwards a tool model gets for free). `Support/BatchFiles.swift` - finding videos and images, file identity, the dated output folder.
- `Sources/Toolbox/Views/` - `RootView`, `TopBar`, `HomeView`, `CategoryView`/`ToolCard`, `ToolView` (shared header + `ToolWorkspace` switch), `Workspaces.swift` (DropZone, Link, Live, Text, OptionsPanel), `FileDrop.swift` (`.fileDrop { urls in … }`: one drop, one call with every file).
- `Tests/ToolboxTests/TestSupport.swift` - shared test helpers: `waitUntil` and `waitUntilReady` (waits that fail at a deadline), `pause(for:because:)`, `Locked` (a value shared with a callback), `killAfterTest` and `killProgramsLeftRunning` (no child process outlives a test), `makeScratchDefaults`, `makeScratchPasteboard`, `element(at:)`, `RGBAPixels` (bounds-checked pixel reads), `missingTools` (skip locally, fail in CI).
- `features/<Category>/<Tool>/README.md` - per-tool brief: design screenshot, sub-features, implementation notes.
- `docs/history/` - dated records (code reviews, plans, handoffs). Each describes the code as it was on its date; check `main` before acting on one. Live plans are GitHub issues.

## How to build a tool
1. Read the tool's README below and open the screenshot it names.
2. Create the view and logic under `Sources/Toolbox/Tools/<Category>/<Tool>/` (separate service type from UI state so it is testable). A tool that takes a list of files builds its model on `BatchQueue` (`Tools/Video/MuteVideo/MuteVideoModel.swift` is the shortest example: its own `Status` and `Item`, a `BatchTool`, and one `queue.run` closure that does the work for one file) and takes drops with `.fileDrop`. Never copy another tool's model or drop handler.
3. Route to it from `ToolWorkspace` in `Views/ToolView.swift`.
4. Run the app and compare against the screenshot (window-only screenshot: `screencapture -l<windowID>`; do not capture the full screen).

## Versioning (every PR that touches the app)
- The version lives in one place: `AppVersion.current` in `Sources/Toolbox/Support/ReleaseNotes.swift` (semantic versioning, `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`). `scripts/make-app.sh` reads it for Info.plist, and the app shows it in the bottom-right corner; clicking it opens the release history.
- Every PR that can change the app bumps it. A PR that changes only files under `.github/`, docs (`.md` files other than `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`), `features/` or `images/` doesn't touch the app and needs no bump; `scripts/app-untouched.sh` is the one list of those paths: PATCH for bug fixes and small tweaks, MINOR for a new tool or feature (resets PATCH), MAJOR only when the user says so. One bump per PR, even with several commits; if main moved on, re-bump above main's version before merging.
- In the same PR add a `Release` at the TOP of `ReleaseNotes.all` with today's date and one plain-words line per change, tagged `.new`, `.improved`, `.fixed` or `.removed`. Write for the user, not the code ("Trim Video keeps the sound", not "fix AVAssetExportSession preset").
- CI enforces it: the "Version bumped" job in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` runs `scripts/check-version-bump.sh`, which fails if the version is not higher than main's or the notes are missing, and passes a branch that leaves the app untouched. Run it locally before opening a PR. `ReleaseNotesTests` checks the history is ordered and matches the current version.
- The full test suite is run locally before every PR (`swift test`), and the PR says so with the exact result line. GitHub's "Build & test" job runs the same suite when a macOS runner is free, but it is not a required check: GitHub's macOS runners often leave it unassigned for 15 minutes and cancel it. A real test failure there is still fixed before merging; an unassigned or cancelled run is not a reason to wait. "Version bumped", "Lint & format" and "Package Toolbox.app" are required.
- A PR that cannot affect the app skips the three macOS jobs. The "Changed paths" job in `ci.yml` decides: the app is unaffected only when every changed path is a `.md` file (except `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`, which is copied into the app), or under `features/` or `images/`, or `.github/dependabot.yml`; any workflow change runs everything. Both decisions read `scripts/app-untouched.sh`, so a docs-only PR needs no version bump and skips the macOS jobs. The checks "Build & test", "Package Toolbox.app" and "Lint & format" still report on every PR: they are ubuntu jobs that pass on the macOS job's success, pass when it was skipped for this reason, and fail otherwise (`.github/scripts/required-check.sh`). When the build starts to read one of those paths, take it off the list in `ci.yml` in the same PR.
- After the PR merges, tag main: `git tag vX.Y.Z && git push origin vX.Y.Z` (ask before pushing). The tag starts `.github/workflows/release.yml`, which checks the tag matches `AppVersion.current` and is on main, builds the ad-hoc signed app with the same action as CI's "Package Toolbox.app" job (`.github/actions/build-app`), and publishes a GitHub release with `Toolbox-X.Y.Z-arm64.zip` and notes made from `ReleaseNotes.swift` (every version since the last release, by `.github/scripts/release-notes.sh`). It never replaces an existing release. A tag that has no release yet is published with `gh workflow run release.yml -f tag=vX.Y.Z`.

## Rules
- Before merging a PR, read its review comments, including Cursor Bugbot's (`gh api repos/stozo04/Mac-Toolbox/pulls/<n>/comments`). Bugbot shows as a "skipping" check, so green checks alone don't mean it found nothing. Fix or answer every finding first.
- On device only; no network/API calls unless a tool's README says otherwise (Download Video, Network Speed Test, the Vault's search model download). Media Info's "Open in Maps" button makes no request from Toolbox, but the `maps.apple.com` link it hands to macOS to open carries the video's coordinates, so they leave the Mac when the user clicks it. The Folio and Scarlet pages' "Get" buttons work the same way: they open the app's download page on GitHub in the browser. The Logs page's "Create GitHub Issue" works the same way: it opens a prefilled `github.com/stozo04/Mac-Toolbox/issues/new` link in the browser (`LogsModel.newIssueURL`), and nothing is sent until the user submits it there. Logs never leave the Mac: `Log` (see `Support/Logging/`) writes every event to `~/Library/Application Support/Toolbox/logs.sqlite` (`LogStore`, kept 90 days), which the **Logs** page shows. Only the installed app uses that file; development runs use `logs-dev.sqlite` (`LogStoreLocation`, `--log-store <file>` in debug builds) and tests a temporary file. Warning/error/critical logs must be detailed enough to diagnose without the user: use `Log.details(error, file:, [context])` (error type/domain/code/description, underlying error, file name/extension/size); `Log` adds the current page and the last events automatically. File names and link paths are fine; never file contents, passwords, tokens, link query strings or the account name (scrubbed by `LogText`). Vault logs drop file names too (`VaultLog`). A weekday 10:00 scheduled task (`scripts/log-fixer/PROMPT.md`) reads new warning+ rows from that file with `sqlite3`, opens a draft PR with fixes, adds the rows that match an open `watch` issue to that issue as evidence, and records each decision in the `log_resolutions` table against the event's `signature` (`LogEvent.signature`), which the Logs page shows as Open, Issue open, Fix proposed, Fixed, Came back and so on. An issue the owner opens from the Logs page is found by the next run (`issue_open`), worked on first, and its PR says `Fixes #N`, so the event moves from the issue to the PR and then to Fixed. The app owns that file's schema. When you add a failure path, log it with `Log.details`.
- Programs are run only through `Shell.run` (`Support/Shell.swift`), which stops them on cancel (throwing `CancellationError`), on timeout (`ShellError.timedOut`) and when the app quits, with SIGTERM and then SIGKILL. Every call states its timeout: `.seconds` (usually `.quick`) for a quick utility, `.untilCancelled` for work that follows the size of the user's file, reports progress or waits for the person. `.untilFinished` is never stopped by a timer, a cancel or quitting; it is only for a write that could damage what it writes if cut off (creating the vault image, changing its password). `Process()` appears nowhere else; the `process_outside_shell` rule in `.swiftlint.yml` enforces it.
- Never modify original user files; write new output. The Mac tools are the deliberate exception: they change system state (Hidden Settings, Presentation Mode, Login Items, Fix "App Is Damaged", Reset Permissions, Uninstall App, Storage Explorer). They never use `sudo`, send removals to the Trash (only the Trash itself is deleted for good, after a confirmation), and ask before anything that weakens a macOS safety check. The Vault page is the other exception, and Download Video's "Add to Vault", which moves its own downloads in the same way: moving a photo or video into the vault deletes the original for good, but only after the copy inside the vault matches it byte for byte and opens; deleting inside the vault is permanent, after a confirmation, except duplicates removed from the Duplicates view, which go to the vault's own Trash inside the encrypted image (Put Back or Empty).
- Tests never subscript a result with a number or force-unwrap one: a wrong count would trap and end the whole run. Use `try XCTUnwrap(x.first)`, `try x.element(at: 1)` or `try XCTUnwrap(value)`. Enforced by the `test_literal_subscript` rule in `.swiftlint.yml` and `force_unwrapping` in `Tests/.swiftlint.yml`.
- Every test wait goes through `waitUntil` (or `waitUntilReady` for an `AVAssetWriterInput`) in `Tests/ToolboxTests/TestSupport.swift`, which fails the test at its deadline. `pause(for:because:)` is only for when there is nothing to observe: a live device settling, or proving that something did not happen a second time. Enforced by `test_unshared_wait`, which bans `Task.sleep` in every other test file.
- Tests leave nothing on the Mac: settings go in `makeScratchDefaults()` (`test_unmanaged_defaults` bans `UserDefaults(suiteName:)` elsewhere), the clipboard is a `makeScratchPasteboard()` handed to the model (`test_general_pasteboard` bans `NSPasteboard.general`), and files go in a temporary folder. The batch tools and Trim Audio make their dated output folder in `~/Downloads`, so a test that runs one creates the model with `outputParent:` set to its temporary folder.
- The Vault search tests that load the real EmbeddingGemma model run only on a Mac that has the model; CI does not download it and skips them. A test that needs the bundled ffmpeg fails in CI when it is missing (`missingTools`) instead of skipping.
- A development run never opens the owner's vault: `VaultLocation.launch` gives it the development folder, tests use temporary folders, and nothing may construct a `VaultModel` or `VaultDoor` on `VaultLocation.standard` except the installed app.
- The vault's disk has one owner: only `VaultDoor` runs `diskutil` or `hdiutil` for it, and nothing else decides whether the vault is open by looking at what is mounted. Ask the door and use the outcome it returns.
- Dropped files are taken with `.fileDrop { urls in … }` (`Views/FileDrop.swift`), which waits for every file of the drop and calls once, so a model sees one drop as one `add`. `.onDrop(of:` appears nowhere else; the `hand_written_file_drop` rule in `.swiftlint.yml` enforces it (`Vault/VaultLibraryView.swift` is excluded until it moves over).
- Icon button that shows a file in Finder: use `RevealButton` (an eye), never a magnifying glass, which reads as search.
- Match the screenshots: dark cards, rounded corners, options panel ~240pt.
- Known gaps: the design shows 87 tools; the catalog has 57 (Battery Health and Replace Audio were dropped on purpose, and the Docs tools were replaced by the Folio card). 9 tools lack a screenshot; their README says so.
- Unbuilt tools show a big red "Feature not implemented" banner (`NotImplementedBanner` in `Views/ToolView.swift`) and a red triangle on their card. `Tool.isImplemented` is derived from `ToolInput.isImplemented` in `Models/Tool.swift`: the generic placeholder cases (`.dropFiles`, `.live`, `.text`) are false, every dedicated case is true, so a tool loses the banner as soon as it gets its own `ToolInput` case. The switch there lists every case and has no `default`, so a new case does not compile until it is put with the placeholders or with the built tools.
- `features/Video/*.md` are the original one-line specs, kept for reference.

- [Display Control](features/Mac/Display%20Control/README.md) - Change the brightness, contrast and volume of external monitors, right from your Mac.
- [Eject All Disks](features/Mac/Eject%20All%20Disks/README.md) - Safely eject every external drive, card and disk image at once before you unplug.
- [Fix App Is Damaged](features/Mac/Fix%20App%20Is%20Damaged/README.md) - Check who made an app that won't open, then clear the download quarantine so macOS lets it run.
- [Hidden Settings](features/Mac/Hidden%20Settings/README.md) - Switch on useful macOS settings that aren't in System Settings, like a faster Dock or hidden files.
- [Keep Awake](features/Mac/Keep%20Awake/README.md) - Stop your Mac from sleeping during downloads, renders or talks, for as long as you choose.
- [Login & Background Items](features/Mac/Login%20%26%20Background%20Items/README.md) - See what starts with your Mac and runs in the background, who made it, and turn off what you don't need.
- [Mouse Fixes](features/Mac/Mouse%20Fixes/README.md) - Scroll your mouse and trackpad in different directions, and make the mouse wheel faster.
- [Network Speed Test](features/Mac/Network%20Speed%20Test/README.md) - Measure download, upload and responsiveness with Apple's own network test.
- [Presentation Mode](features/Mac/Presentation%20Mode/README.md) - One switch before you share your screen: hide desktop icons, hide the Dock and stay awake.
- [Reset Permissions](features/Mac/Reset%20Permissions/README.md) - Unstick an app's Screen Recording, Microphone, Camera or other privacy access.
- [Storage Explorer](features/Mac/Storage%20Explorer/README.md) - See what's filling your disk, find the usual space hogs and clear them out safely.
- [Uninstall App](features/Mac/Uninstall%20App/README.md) - Remove an app together with the caches, preferences and data it leaves behind.
- [Wi-Fi Password & QR](features/Mac/Wi-Fi%20Password%20%26%20QR/README.md) - Show the password of a saved Wi-Fi network and make a QR code guests can scan to join.
